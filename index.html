What are Miller indices?

Three integers that name a family of parallel planes in a crystal. They are worked out from where the plane cuts the x, y and z axes.

( h k l )
  • h comes from the x-axis intercept
  • k comes from the y-axis intercept
  • l comes from the z-axis intercept

Why we use them

  • To name and tell apart planes in a crystal
  • To describe how a plane is oriented
  • To calculate the spacing between planes
  • To interpret X-ray diffraction patterns
  • Introduced by W. H. Miller in 1839

A plane parallel to an axis never meets it. Its intercept is ∞, so its reciprocal is 0.

Spacing between planes

In a cubic crystal with lattice constant a, neighbouring (hkl) planes are separated by:

d = a / √(h² + k² + l²)
Planeh² + k² + l²d
(100)1a
(110)2a/√2 ≈ 0.707a
(111)3a/√3 ≈ 0.577a

This d is the same one that appears in Bragg's law, nλ = 2d sinθ.

Rules to remember

Brackets
(hkl) is one plane, {hkl} is a family of equivalent planes, [uvw] is a direction.
Parallel to an axis
The intercept is infinite, so that index is zero.
Negative index
Write a bar over the number: (1 1 0) is read "one, bar one, zero".
Whole numbers
Reduce to the smallest whole-number set. Parallel planes share the same indices.

Conclusion

Miller indices give a simple, mathematical way to describe the orientation of planes inside a crystal.
